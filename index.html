About My Research

I am a PhD Scholar in the Department of Mechanical Engineering at the Indian Institute of Technology Bombay. While formally affiliated with IIT Bombay, I operate with the ethos of an independent researcher. My work is driven by a deep, self-directed curiosity to bridge fundamental theoretical mechanics with applied computational frameworks.

My primary research spans nonlinear ultrasonics, mechanical metamaterials, and inverse design. I specialize in developing numerical frameworks (FEM, spring-mass chains) to understand complex wave propagation, harmonic scattering, and early-stage damage detection. I am particularly fascinated by cross-disciplinary physics—such as drawing analogies between acoustic wave scattering and optical Raman scattering to engineer "elastically invisible" metamaterials.

As an independent researcher, I prioritize open, machine-readable science. Through this archive and my Google Site, I aim to provide a fully transparent, globally accessible record of my publications, conference presentations, and continuous research journey.

Concise Journey Through My Research

I am an independent Research Scholar at IIT Bombay, exploring theoretical, computational, and experimental mechanics. My research focuses on nonlinear waves in solids, unraveling phenomena like harmonic generation and scattering. I aim to quantify early-stage damage for structural health monitoring, including super-resolution imaging. Simultaneously, I design linear and nonlinear metamaterials to control elastic waves. My vision is to integrate diverse research domains for innovative solutions in structural damage detection and monitoring, forging a transformative research universe in nonlinear elastic metamaterials.

Comprehensive Research Journey

I am privileged to serve as an independent Research Scholar at the Indian Institute of Technology Bombay (IIT Bombay), where my academic pursuits revolve around the captivating realm of theoretical, computational, and experimental mechanics.

My research endeavors encompass a broad spectrum of exploration, spanning theoretical, computational, and experimental facets. Currently, I am immersed in a dynamic research journey centered on nonlinear waves within solids. This compelling pursuit delves into phenomena as intriguing as harmonic generation, wave mixing, and harmonic scattering, all viewed through the lenses of both theory and computation. My aspiration is to unravel the intricate physics underpinning these nonlinear effects, ultimately empowering us to quantify early-stage damages at the micro-meso scale within solid materials. This breakthrough not only aids in monitoring structural component health but also promises groundbreaking applications in structural and biomedical health monitoring, including the cutting-edge realm of super-resolution imaging. Notably, my research involves the development of bespoke experimental setups from scratch, unveiling the fascinating intricacies of nonlinear wave phenomena within metallic materials.

In parallel, my research takes an innovative turn as I embark on the design and control of linear and nonlinear metamaterials, employing a pioneering inverse design approach. These metamaterials are meticulously crafted to exert precise command over linear and nonlinear elastic waves, functioning adeptly within both the time and frequency domains. The characteristics of these inversely designed metamaterials are diverse, encompassing attributes such as multi-material compositions, periodic and quasi-periodic structures, direction-dependent and direction-independent properties, mode-independent behavior, concealed nonlinear inclusions, and temporal stretching pulses. My research extends to encompass forward design explorations of spatially intricate, functionally graded metamaterials and temporally evolving, 4D metamaterials. These investigations promise to unlock novel responses and functionalities, fueling advances in elastic wave manipulation.

My overarching vision is to seamlessly integrate multiple research domains to drive innovation in the field of health monitoring. I aspire to foster synergies between disparate areas, including Metamaterials, Nonlinear Waves, Inverse Design, Nonlinear Ultrasonics, Physical Acoustics, AI/ML for Structural Health Monitoring, NDE 4.0, IoT, 3D Printing/Additive Manufacturing, Composite Materials, Impact Mechanics, and Fracture Mechanics. By harnessing the collective potential of these domains, my aim is to pioneer innovative solutions for the early detection and monitoring of structural damages.

This journey marks the inception of a transformative research universe, one that revolves around nonlinear elastic metamaterials—a universe poised to redefine the boundaries of elastic wave manipulation.

I am enthusiastic about forging collaborations and connecting with like-minded individuals and organizations committed to advancing knowledge in the field of mechanics and beyond. Together, we can drive pioneering discoveries and innovations that will shape the future of health monitoring and structural integrity.

Nonlinear Effects of Linear Time-Dependent Metamaterials (META 2023)

META 2023 Paris – Oral Presentation
13th International Conference on Metamaterials, Photonic Crystals and Plasmonics

Scientific Contribution: This study demonstrates that linear time-dependent elastic metamaterials can generate nonlinear effects. Traditional nonlinear metamaterials rely on nonlinear constitutive behavior. In contrast, this work shows that dynamic temporal modulation of otherwise linear material properties can induce harmonic generation and enable control over frequency combinations under monochromatic wave and two-wave mixing.

Personal Milestone: META 2023 was my first in-person international conference, attended on merit with complete funding and scholarship support. The conference featured plenary and keynote lectures by distinguished researchers including Prof. Jeremy Baumberg (University of Cambridge), Prof. Päivi Törmä (Aalto University), Prof. Harry Atwater (Caltech), Prof. Nikolay Zheludev (University of Southampton), Prof. Marin Soljačić (MIT), Prof. Jelena Vuckovic (Stanford University), and Prof. Federico Capasso (Harvard University). I also had the privilege of an extended research discussion with Prof. Sir John Pendry (Imperial College London), widely regarded as the father of metamaterials and recipient of the 2024 Kyoto Prize. Our discussion lasted around 20–30 minutes.

The Reading Room

If you were to judge a mind by its bookshelf, this grid is a map of my internal landscape. It is a collection built on a simple premise: reality is complicated, and I want to understand it.

There are the guides for the journey inward. Books like The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ck and The Courage to Be Disliked serve as emotional armor, while Do Epic Shit and Focus on What Matters are the battle cries for daily discipline. To find stillness in the noise, I turn to Quiet and The Art of Being Alone, seeking the kind of peace that Cal Newport describes in Digital Minimalism, or the simple joy of Ikigai.

But a mind needs fuel. That’s where the scientists come in. Richard Feynman reminds me to stay curious with Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!, while Chaos and We Have No Idea keep me humble about the universe’s mysteries. The Almanack of Naval Ravikant and Elon Musk offer blueprints for building a future, while The Diary of a Gym Addict and Nonlinear Acoustics remind me that mastery requires sweat, both physical and intellectual.

And for the moments when words need to be weaponized or savored, I have 100 Quotes That Will Change Your Life. This isn't just a reading list; it's a toolkit for living a life of purpose, curiosity, and quiet strength.

The Arena

Sport, for me, is not a pastime. It is a mirror.

At the center of this mirror stands a boy from Rosario who became the world. Lionel Messi — not just a footballer, but a lifelong lesson in patience, genius, and quiet rebellion. I have watched him walk the pitch at Camp Nou with the number 10 on his back, and I have watched him stand alone in the rain, head bowed, wondering if the universe would ever be kind. I have seen him kiss the World Cup, hold it like a child, lift it in the Qatari night while the bisht draped his shoulders and the world finally, finally, exhaled. I have seen him with his family on the podium, his sons in Argentina blue, his wife beside him — the man behind the myth. And I have seen him fall, tackled, broken, human. That is why he matters. Because he rose again.

But this section is not only about Messi. It is about the moments that made me love sport itself. It is Sachin Tendulkar, bat raised, a billion prayers behind him. It is Messi sitting with his seven Ballon d'Or trophies like a king at rest. It is MS Dhoni — Thala — calm in the chaos, finishing matches like he was born to do it. It is Roger Federer, elegant even in defeat, holding tennis balls like they were always his. It is the raw discipline of karate, the white gi, the controlled violence of mastery. It is Pep Guardiola laughing, José Mourinho grinning with a caption that reads "Is it April Fools Day?" — men who turned tactics into theatre. It is Suárez, the warrior, in Atlético red and Barcelona blue.

And then there are the badges. Manchester City. FC Barcelona. Chennai Super Kings. Liverpool. PSG. Each one a chapter of belonging, a color of memory. Some of these clubs gave me joy. Some gave me heartbreak. All of them gave me something to feel. This is not a highlight reel. It is a love letter to the beautiful game, and to every sport that ever made my heart race.

The Silver Screen Chronicles

If music is my heartbeat, cinema is the sprawling, unending universe I escape to. My taste in movies is not bound by language, geography, or genre—it is bound only by the ability to rearrange my soul. This isn't just a list; it is a highly curated, deeply personal archive of my life's greatest cinematic loves.

At the absolute pinnacle of this universe stands John Wick. All of them. From the first film to Chapter 4, it is a symphony of choreographed violence, neon-drenched aesthetics, and world-building so rich it feels mythological. Right beside it, breathing the same rarefied air of ultimate cool, is Vikram. Lokesh Kanagaraj's masterpiece is a collision of legends—Kamal Haasan, Vijay Sethupathi, and Fahadh Faasil. And then, the tone shifts from adrenaline to obsession with Whiplash. "Not quite my tempo." It is a brutal, blood-sweat-and-tears rhythm of artistic obsession.

From there, the universe expands into the boundless possibilities of Hollywood. I find the intellectual bite of The Social Network and Good Will Hunting, the human resilience of The Shawshank Redemption, and the quiet ache of Manchester by the Sea and (500) Days of Summer. Gifted and Coco remind me that the simplest stories about family, memory, and belonging can leave the deepest impressions, while La La Land turns love and ambition into something beautifully bittersweet.

But my heart is undeniably rooted in Indian cinema, and Kannada cinema holds a sacred, foundational place in it. It is the raw, gritty realism of Lucia and Ugramm, the mass euphoria of KGF, and the deeply emotional worlds of Mungaru Male and Raajakumara. It is the legacy of Om, the cultural and natural world celebrated in Gandhada Gudi, and the pure, heart-wrenching joy of 777 Charlie. Then there is the fiercely grounded, deeply honest world of Marathi cinema: Ved, Shala, Deool, Mumbai-Pune-Mumbai, and the towering heights of Natsamrat.

The Telugu and Tamil waves are a tidal force of storytelling. I lose myself in the epic scale of Baahubali, RRR, and Kalki 2898 AD. I cheer for the underdog in Jersey and Salaar, feel the youthful romance of Sita Ramam and Hi Nanna, and laugh until it hurts with Jathi Ratnalu and Tillu. Tamil cinema brings a different kind of intensity: the raw energy of Vikram Vedha, Kaithi, Maharaja, and Sarpatta Parambarai.

I am a polyglot of emotions. In Hindi cinema, I find myself in the timeless nostalgia of Kal Ho Naa Ho, Kabhi Khushi Kabhie Gham, and Rehnaa Hai Terre Dil Mein. And then there is Malayalam cinema, an industry I admire for its remarkable ability to move between intimate human stories and intricately crafted thrillers: from the youthful romance of Premam and Hridayam to the grounded intensity of Kannur Squad and the masterclass in suspense that is Drishyam.

The Episodic Campfire

If movies are the grand, sweeping novels of cinema, web series are the intimate, serialized chapters of our lives. They are the comfort food we binge when the world feels too heavy, and the adrenaline shots we mainline when we need to escape.

It begins with the holy trinity of Indian internet content—the shows that made us feel seen. Pitchers, with its raw dream of startup glory. Kota Factory, which captured the trauma, pressure, and unexpected friendships of the IIT-JEE grind. Panchayat, a quiet masterpiece about a city boy finding his purpose in a sleepy village, and Gullak, the heartwarming saga of a middle-class family. This is the world of Yeh Meri Family, Cubicles, and What The Folks—shows that find extraordinary magic in ordinary lives.

Then, there are the shows that define an era of storytelling. Rocket Boys is a triumph of ambition, science, and history. Little Things is the modern, deeply comforting portrait of a live-in relationship. But the binge-watching doesn't stop at comfort. I need the edge of my seat. The Family Man and Aarya bring the intense, gritty reality of espionage and crime. Asur, Lupin, Money Heist, Bose: Dead/Alive, and Illegal are masterclasses in tension, heists, and psychological warfare.

And I love the regional, quirky gems that break the mold. Aani Kay Hava brings Marathi warmth and wit. I find myself lost in the laughs of Perm. Roommates, Save The Tigers, and Female Flatmate. This section is a reminder that stories don't need to be condensed into three hours to change your life. Sometimes, they need to unfold slowly, week by week, leaving you eagerly waiting for the next chapter.

🔬 Research Profile, Institutional Roles, Indexing & Media Layer

🧭 Research at a Glance — The Story Behind the Record

The work documented across this archive spans nonlinear ultrasonics, mechanical metamaterials, and inverse design. The throughline is a single question: how can we make early-stage damage visible, and how can we engineer materials that hide or reveal elastic waves on demand? Answering it required a three-part research program — theoretical foundations, computational inverse design, and hands-on experimental validation — each represented in the tables and links below.

Foundational nonlinear elastodynamics. Starting with rate-independent hysteretic damage models (Bouc-Wen, Reid's pinched hysteresis, two-state formulations), the work established new analytical and numerical results on how harmonic scattering emerges from localized micro-defects — including the discovery that asymmetric contact interfaces activate both odd and even higher harmonics, overturning a decades-old assumption (ICSV 2021, FWCI 1.51). These results now underpin later inverse-design frameworks.

Inverse design & metamaterials. Building on the foundational results, the research shifted to designing rather than just diagnosing: gradient-free optimization, level-set shape optimization, and transient finite-element formulations were combined to synthesize direction-dependent, multi-material, and functionally graded metamaterials capable of selectively suppressing or amplifying specific harmonic bands. Journal articles documenting this arc appear across JASA (Vols. 148–156) — their DOIs are indexed in the Publisher DOIs category of the 250+ Link Directory above, and their full reference records appear in Category 4 and Category 5.

Experimental & instrumentation. Every computational result was designed to be verifiable. Bespoke experimental setups for nonlinear ultrasonic measurement (RITEC GA-2500A amplifier, Olympus V-109/V-110 transducers, Tektronix scopes) were built from scratch, alongside Hopkinson Pressure Bar impact characterization of composite laminates and dynamic photoelasticity for Mode I crack propagation — Figshare DOIs for these datasets are indexed in Category 1 of the Link Directory.

The 828-page single-author PhD thesis (IIT Bombay, June 2024, 2% Turnitin similarity) is the consolidated record of this journey. Its public Figshare DOI disclosures appear in Category 1. All 11 peer-reviewed journal articles and 2 research posters are linked in Categories 4 and 5 of the Link Directory above.
